Issues & Data

Understand the biodiversity crisis, access authoritative data sources, and learn about monitoring limitations.

Critical Issues Facing SDG 15

🔴 Deforestation & forest loss

Status: CRITICAL — Tropical forests are cleared at ~10M hectares/year for agriculture, logging, cattle ranching, and development. The Amazon alone loses an area the size of a football field every minute.

Impact: Carbon release, species habitat loss, indigenous land displacement, reduced rainfall patterns.

🔴 Biodiversity collapse

Status: CRITICAL — 1 million animal and plant species face extinction. Extinction rates are 1,000x the natural baseline. Vertebrate populations have declined 68% since 1970.

Drivers: Habitat destruction, climate change, pollution, overexploitation, invasive species.

🟠 Climate change impact

Status: HIGH — Rising temperatures alter ecosystems faster than species can adapt. Coral bleaching, shifting migration patterns, and extreme weather events accelerate biodiversity loss.

Vulnerable areas: Arctic ecosystems, coral reefs, montane forests, small island states.

🟠 Desertification & land degradation

Status: HIGH — 1.3 billion people depend on degrading drylands. Soil loss, salinization, and vegetation loss reduce agricultural productivity and increase poverty.

Affected regions: Sub-Saharan Africa, Central Asia, Middle East, parts of China and India.

🟠 Wildlife trafficking & poaching

Status: HIGH — Illegal wildlife trade worth $23B annually drives species toward extinction. African elephants, rhinoceros, pangolins, and tigers face critical pressure.

Solutions: Demand reduction, community protection, international enforcement (CITES).

🔵 Invasive species

Status: MODERATE — Invasive species cost the global economy $120B+ annually and outcompete native species. Examples: Burmese pythons in Florida, Asian carp in North America.

Data Limitations & Caveats

⚠️ Important limitations

  • Geographic bias: Developed countries have better monitoring infrastructure. Tropical regions often lack comprehensive data despite highest biodiversity.
  • Temporal lag: Satellite data updates quarterly or annually. Real-time monitoring is incomplete.
  • Incomplete species inventory: ~80% of species remain undiscovered. GBIF data reflects observed and recorded occurrences, not complete distributions.
  • Protected area effectiveness: Designation doesn't guarantee protection. Enforcement, funding, and management quality vary widely.
  • Climate projections: Future species range shifts depend on climate models with inherent uncertainty.
  • Indigenous knowledge: Western scientific monitoring often undervalues traditional ecological knowledge held by indigenous communities.

How to use this data responsibly

  • Always cite original sources — use links to data providers above.
  • Combine multiple datasets — no single source is complete.
  • Account for measurement uncertainty in models and projections.
  • Engage with indigenous communities and local experts when working in biodiverse regions.
  • Update analyses as new data becomes available (quarterly for forest data, annual for most biodiversity data).
